Display Advertising

Search ads reach people who are already looking. Display ads reach people who aren't looking yet. This page covers how the auction works, who to target, what makes an ad worth clicking, and what the research says about retargeting.

Ad networks

Millions of publishers have ad space. Millions of advertisers want attention. Instead of billions of one-to-one deals, each side works with one network, which runs an auction every time a page loads.

Without the network, every advertiser would need a separate deal with every website. Ask yourself: which side pays for the free news site you read this morning?

Display ad bids

Three ways to pay. They all convert into each other once you know the click-through rate (CTR) and conversion rate.

CPM

Cost per mille

Pay per 1,000 impressions. You carry the risk that nobody clicks.

CPC

Cost per click

Pay only when someone clicks. The risk is in whether they buy.

CPA

Cost per acquisition

Pay per conversion. The cost that matters for profit.

CPC = CPM ÷ (1,000 × CTR)
CPA = CPC ÷ Conversion rate

Display ad targeting

Google Ads lets you choose who sees an ad. Affinity tells you who someone is. In-market tells you what they're about to buy.

Demographics

Age, gender, parental status, household income.

e.g. parents of toddlers

Affinity

Long-term interests and habits. Best for building awareness.

e.g. golf enthusiasts

In-market

People actively researching a purchase right now.

e.g. in-market for SUVs

Life events

Major moments that change what people buy.

e.g. recently moved, getting married

Your data

Past site visitors and your customer lists. This is retargeting.

e.g. abandoned a cart last week

Custom segments

You define the audience with keywords, URLs or apps.

e.g. people who search "standing desk"

Combined segments

Mix segments with AND / OR logic.

e.g. in-market for cars AND parents
Update: Google retired "similar segments" in 2023. Optimized targeting and audience expansion now find people who resemble your customers. Category names in Google Ads change often, so check the live menu.

What makes a display ad work

The consumer has to get through four steps, and each step loses people. Good ad copy keeps as many as possible moving.

The four steps

1 · See the ad
2 · Process the ad
3 · Decide to click
4 · Convert

Watch for "banner blindness": people learn to ignore spots where ads usually appear.

Retargeting: does specific beat generic?

Dynamic retargeting shows you the exact product you viewed. Generic retargeting shows a brand ad. Lambrecht and Tucker (2013) tested both with a travel site, assigning visitors at random to see one or the other.

Broad vs. narrow construal

"Beach vacation."

Early in the decision: thinking is broad. A broad, inspiring ad matches.

"A hotel on the north side of the island with direct beach access and a pool that overlooks the waves."

Late in the decision: thinking is narrow. A specific, product-level ad matches.

Match the ad's level of detail to the customer's level of detail.

Similar audiences

The only difference between green and purple people is ad exposure. Green people show you what a buyer looks like.

Spend on purple people who resemble green people, not on more impressions to blue people who already said no.

Consistent messaging

Never Start A Marketing Campaign Without A Dedicated Landing Page.

The landing page's first job is to tell the visitor, "Yes, you're in the right place." Route each click to the page that matches what the person clicked on: a logo goes to the brand page, a specific product goes to that product.

Coastline Hotels · Ad

Three things to match

1
Same value proposition. If the ad says "works offline," the page leads with working offline.
2
Same call to action. "Book now" in the ad means a booking box on the page.
3
Same design. Colors, fonts, characters, imagery.
Case: the laptop ad that matched the look but not the message
The ad promised getting work done without Wi-Fi. The landing page matched the colors, logo placement and clean layout, but the headline was a general "it does everything" message. The offline benefit, the reason people clicked, was missing.
Case: the hotel ad that matched almost everything
An animated hotel ad used a "summer isn't over" headline, a 15% member discount and a "Book now" button. The landing page repeated the headline lettering, the discount, the member requirement and a booking box. Only a decorative sun was missing, which matters far less than the message and offer.

Exploring affinity audiences

Affinity segments go several levels deep. The deeper you go, the smaller and more specific the audience. Use interests to find a type of customer, not just a fan of the topic.

Key terms

Publisher
A site or app that sells ad space.
Ad network
The marketplace connecting publishers and advertisers and running the auctions.
CPM / CPC / CPA
Cost per thousand impressions, per click and per acquisition.
CTR
Click-through rate: clicks ÷ impressions.
Break-even CPM
1,000 × CTR × conversion rate × profit per conversion. Bid above it and you lose money.
Affinity audience
People grouped by long-term interests and habits.
In-market audience
People actively researching a purchase.
Retargeting
Showing ads to people who already visited your site.
Dynamic retargeting
Retargeting with the specific product the person viewed.
Construal level
How broad or specific someone's thinking is at a point in their decision.
Obtrusive ad
An ad that interrupts: pop-ups, auto-play video, page takeovers.
Landing page
The page a visitor reaches after clicking an ad; it should continue the ad's message.
